Wellbeing apps ask for homework

Plenty of men carry a lot and say nothing. The wellbeing apps I tried ask for work in return: sign up, check in, keep a streak, log your mood, write it down. And they talk like a leaflet. For someone who already feels like everything is on him, that’s one more job.

  • Sign up
  • Keep a streak
  • Track your mood
  • Do your daily homework

Just the words, where he’ll see them.

So the app had to

  • Ask for nothingNo account, no streaks, no check-ins. Set it up once.
  • Sound like a mateStraight, warm and a bit British. Never clinical.
  • Come to himOn the Home Screen and Lock Screen, without opening the app.

The widget is the product

If the words have to be opened, they won’t be read. So the main experience sits on the Home Screen and the Lock Screen, and the app is where you shape it. That decision came first and everything else followed it.

Two questions, then out of the way

Setup is two questions long. Nothing is asked that the app doesn’t need, and you can skip straight to a message.

  1. Lead with the voiceThe name does the talking. Set it up, or skip straight to a message.

Rules written into the code

For an app like this, trust is the product. Some decisions aren’t preferences, they’re rules that every release has to keep.

  • No tracking.No analytics or advertising code, no accounts. Nothing a person chooses or saves leaves the phone.
  • “Go easy” never shows a hard message.Checked by an automated test, so a gentle setting can’t surprise someone on a bad day.
  • Support numbers are re-checked before every release.The helplines on the Support screen, for England, have to be right every time.
  • Free, for good.No subscriptions, no adverts, no paywalls. Encouragement shouldn’t come with a price tag.

You Got This, Bro. offers encouragement and general wellbeing support. It isn’t a substitute for professional care, and the app says so.
